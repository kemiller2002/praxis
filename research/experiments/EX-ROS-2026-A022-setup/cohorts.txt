2. Where each cohort comes from
-------------------------------
High affinity: the "Local control-plane API and host" requirement family of
requirements/EXECUTION-ORCHESTRATION.md (PRX-CTL-001..012 with PRX-UI-009,
-024, -027; status Proposed; tracked by #94/#96; listed as not yet implemented
in docs/execution-runtime.md). It is a different subsystem from the A021
work-group commands (HTTP host, typed control-plane contract, executions and
receipts, hub) and is implementable in F# inside this repository on top of
the existing `praxis web serve` and `praxis hub serve`. The designer split the
family into work items along requirement-ID boundaries (one item per
requirement cluster, each citing its PRX IDs) and captured them at the
baseline. That the designer wrote these items is a threat to validity
(section 6), as it was in A021.

Low affinity: pre-existing items from five unrelated subsystems, four of them
captured by other executions before this experiment was conceived, plus one
item captured from a long-open issue (#56). Their acceptance criteria were
made explicit at the baseline from their own descriptions; each original
description is preserved verbatim ahead of the added "Acceptance:" text.
They are not trivial or defective work: PRAXIS-STATE-MERGE-01 is the
repository's highest-priority open item, and each item fixes a confirmed,
still-present defect or an acknowledged gap.

Why the high cohort is high (summary of the matrix): every pair shares the
control-plane host files, the typed control-plane contract and route family,
and at least one strong dimension; the members share the invariants "no
second canonical store", "converge on the CLI transition path" and, for the
read items, "read endpoints never write"; PRAXIS-CTL-04 defines the refusal
model PRAXIS-CTL-02 reports; PRAXIS-CTL-05 imposes a fingerprint and identity
on every other member's responses; PRAXIS-CTL-06 consumes PRAXIS-CTL-02's
contract. A cohort-level design (one envelope, one refusal model, one way of
reading state through the CLI) is plausibly valuable.

Why the low cohort is low: the members touch five different stores and
module trees; 9 of 10 pairs share no dimension at all beyond generic files;
the exceptions are one shared file (src/Ros.Domain/Artifacts/Policy.fs for
ACTOR-KIND-ANCHOR x PRAXIS-CN-GL-KINDS) and one shared store
(.ros/events/events.jsonl, read by ATTR-RECONCILE-SYMLINK-SUBMODULE,
merged by PRAXIS-STATE-MERGE-01). No item's design needs another's.

5. Expected implementation scope
--------------------------------
High: each member extends src/Ros.Cli/WebInterface.fs, WebHttp.fs, Hub.fs and
CliProcess.fs and adds typed contracts (expected under src/Ros.Contracts), with
tests in tests/Ros.Tests/WebInterfaceTests.fs and HubTests.fs and docs in
docs/web-interface.md and docs/project-administration-hub.md. Expected size
per member: medium (one to a few hundred source lines plus tests).
Low: each member stays in its own subsystem (see observations.json 'files').
PRAXIS-STATE-MERGE-01 is the largest item of either cohort.

6. Possible confounders (also in the experiment record, "Threats")
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Designer-authored high cohort: the designer captured PRAXIS-CTL-02..06 and
  wrote their criteria; the low cohort is mostly pre-existing text.
- Complexity and affinity overlap: the high cohort is five medium items in a
  new surface; the low cohort mixes small fixes and one substantial item.
  Criteria counts are close (27 and 26) but per-item difficulty differs.
- Novelty: the high cohort builds a new API surface (more design freedom,
  more room for cross-item inconsistency); the low cohort mostly fixes
  existing behaviour (less design freedom). Some of the expected
  consistency difference is therefore built into the cohorts, which is part
  of what "affinity" means here and is reported as such.
- One shared file and one shared store inside the low cohort (section 3).
- PRAXIS-CTL-01 and PRAXIS-TELEMETRY-CLASSIFY-VOCAB remain ready in the
  baseline backlog with the same "not to be merged" note; an implementer may
  notice them. The prompts restrict each condition to its own items.
- The baseline's live context contains one active work item of the setup
  itself (EXP-A022-BASELINE; it completes on the setup branch, not in the
  arms) besides the pre-existing test-data items WI-READY (active) and
  WI-ACTIVE (blocked). Implementers must not touch any of them; the prompts
  say so.
