# Evidence Record

## Evidence summary

Established engineering disciplines and professions exhibit combinations of
independently governed bodies of knowledge, education and accreditation,
competence assessment, professional community, standards, ethical duties,
independent practice, reproducible evidence, and economic utility.

FE currently has internal research and tooling analogues but no demonstrated
independent community, accredited curriculum, competency assessment,
professional code, credentialing route, field standard, or external outcome
base.

## Exact claim supported or contradicted

Supports the claim that FE should not currently be presented as an
established engineering discipline.

## Source provenance

Comparison of:

- ABET 2026–2027 accreditation criteria;
- SEBoK and IEEE SWEBOK bodies of knowledge;
- Engineering Council UK-SPEC competence expectations;
- NSPE ethical duties;
- direct FE repository observation.

Source URLs and limitations are preserved under EV-FEB-001, 005, and 028–030
in the Framework Engineering source registry.

## Relevant excerpt or data

FE passes an internal coherence gate and partially passes reproducibility,
boundary, governance, and pilot-release gates. It does not pass comparative
utility, transfer, institutional-practice, or discipline gates.

## Interpretation

Discipline formation is not a documentation milestone. It requires durable,
independent, socially accountable practice and demonstrated outcomes. Those
are downstream of FE's present research gate.

## Limitations

The exemplars are not asserted to form a universal or legally binding
discipline checklist. Institutional maturity also does not by itself prove a
field's scientific validity.

## Counterevidence

FE's repository contains a substantial corpus, provisional theory, governance
rules, experiments, and tools. These support continued research and could
become inputs to later institutional development.
